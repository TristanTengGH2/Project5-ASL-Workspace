## Ideation

### Ideas

Ideas considered (from the brainstorming chat, Oct–Nov 2026), all web apps:

- **Adaptive music maker / interaction engine.** Children with limited movement build music with configurable large targets. Appeals: clear accessibility purpose. Concern: needs therapist input; less novel technically.
- **Rehabilitation activity companion (cerebral palsy).** Guided real-world practice with a story. Concern: therapeutic claims need clinical review.
- **Interactive webcam mirror.** Body tracking becomes visual or musical feedback. Concern: needs a clear purpose.
- **ASL translation, learning, and annotation workspace.** Webcam hand tracking recognizes signs, supports learning, and lets users review recordings. Appeals: webcam tracking is the Developer's main interest; a real communication need. Concern: recognition accuracy and data licensing.
- **Sound-awareness / visual turn-taking tools.** Concern: existing products (e.g. Apple Sound Recognition) already cover much of this.

### Choice

Chosen: a free web app where people can **translate** (recognize) supported signs, **look up** an unknown sign, and **learn** signs. Priorities: Translate and unknown-sign lookup first; Learn second; review/annotation later. Why: webcam tracking is the Developer's main interest, and anyone with a webcam can use it. Set aside: the music and rehabilitation ideas (need clinician input) and sound-awareness (crowded space).

Competition: webcam-only ASL learning tools already exist (e.g. [Signpost](https://signpost.cv/), [ASL Pal](https://aslpalpilotprogram.com/), [PopSign](https://signdata.cc.gatech.edu/view/datasets/popsign_v1_0/)), and feature-based reverse dictionaries exist (e.g. [Handspeak](https://www.handspeak.com/word/asl-eng/)). Camera-based lookup appeared only in research prototypes in my searches ([2021](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-70650-0_14), [2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13571v1)); I found no free public consumer version, but I have not checked app stores. This is a possible gap, not a proven one.

### Branding

Working title: _Handshape_ (Developer's marketing name; handshape is one of five ASL parameters, so copy should not claim ASL "is based in" it). Direction to explore: calm, high-contrast, video-first, large readable type, captions on everything. Check for collisions with existing products before finalizing.

## Pitch

### Concept

A free browser app, with no account or special equipment beyond a webcam, built around a database of verified ASL signs. Hand tracking runs locally and follows hand shape, location, and movement over time. Users can (1) perform a supported sign and see what the model recognizes, with honest uncertainty; (2) look up a sign they don't know, getting ranked candidates with reference videos; (3) learn signs by comparing their attempt with a reference. The app recognizes isolated signs; it does not translate ASL conversation.

### Title and branding

Working title: _Handshape_. Logo and visual rules go in `spec.md`.

### Why this project

I chose this because it reminds me of my friend's sister, who is deaf. I've seen how hard it is, and how limited communication is, for people who don't have easy access to ASL or to someone who can check that they're signing it properly. I want an easier way to make sure a sign is right without needing another human to check you. Webcam motion tracking is also the part of this project I most want to explore.

Other tools already teach ASL with a webcam, and some dictionaries let you look up a sign by its parts, so I'm not claiming a gap. My aims are a free tool anyone can use with just a webcam, a way to look up a sign you've seen but don't know ([a documented learner problem](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05857v1)), and honest reporting of what the model can and can't tell you. It can show whether the model recognized the sign I meant. It cannot judge whether my form, facial expression, or grammar is fully correct, and it does not replace a teacher or interpreter.

### Users

Hearing people (family, friends, classmates) learning ASL to communicate with Deaf signers. They need to look up unfamiliar signs, check a sign they practiced, and know the tool's limits. Not all Deaf or hard-of-hearing people use ASL. This is a learning aid, not a replacement for interpreters.

### Experience goals

Honest and low-pressure. Users start the camera, see their hands tracked, perform or describe a sign, and read ranked results with their uncertainty. The supported vocabulary is always visible. A tracking problem never reads as a signing mistake.

### Technical goals

- Two-hand webcam tracking in the browser, processed locally, with timestamped landmark sequences so hand location and larger movements are captured, not just handshape.
- A sequence model trained on a verified, licensed dataset; measured accuracy on unseen signers; "unknown" handling; latency on the target device.
- Ranked top-K output, so lookup works even when top-1 is wrong.
- Handshape and location filters from ASL-LEX data.
- Capture, tracking, features, and model kept in separate modules behind a replaceable model interface.
- No LLM at launch. Lookup uses the sign model plus handshape and location filters. Decision: build filters first and compare; if filters work well enough, skip the LLM.

### Starting point

From scratch. Data basis (licenses to be confirmed before use):

| Source | Use | Verified so far | Open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [PopSign v1.0](https://signdata.cc.gatech.edu/view/datasets/popsign_v1_0/) | Leading candidate for training | 250 signs, 47 Deaf signers, 200,686 videos (1.1 TB); license page states CC BY 4.0; documented as suitable for isolated sign recognition on phones, unsuitable for continuous recognition or sign-to-English translation | Raw video is 1.1 TB, so download and landmark extraction need a plan; largely one-handed signs |
| [ASL Citizen](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/asl-citizen/) | Possible supplement for evaluation | 83,399 videos, 2,731 signs, 52 signers; consented under IRB approval with Deaf research team members; framed by its authors as dictionary retrieval (the same task as this app's lookup); baseline about 62% top-1 per the project page (the paper summary I saw said 63%, so check the paper), 91% recall@10 per the paper ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05934)) | [License](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/asl-citizen/dataset-license/): non-commercial research only, no redistribution of the data, grant-back to Microsoft; unclear on trained models |
| [ASL-LEX 2.0](https://osf.io/zpha4/) | Sign metadata (handshape, location, movement) | 2,723 signs, peer-reviewed ([Sehyr et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977685)); OSF lists CC BY 4.0 | Whether its video clips fall under the same license |
| MediaPipe | Hand tracking | [Apache 2.0](https://github.com/google/mediapipe/blob/master/LICENSE) | None |

Avoided: **WLASL**, whose labels come from video titles and show [documented gloss inconsistency](https://open.bu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/3bf02cc0-5fce-4afa-b8e0-8dbd28822e92/content).

### Tech stack

Confirmed by the Developer (Nov 2). TypeScript + Vite; MediaPipe Tasks Vision; Python for landmark extraction and training; a small model exported to the browser (TensorFlow.js or ONNX Runtime Web); static hosting (GitHub Pages). Python/ML training and browser model export are new to me.

### MVP or critical function

Riskiest first: webcam, two-hand tracking, a deliberate Start/Finish attempt, then a real ranked prediction (top-5) for about 20–30 signs, with "unknown" at low confidence. It works when held-out-signer accuracy is measured and reported, and ordinary non-signing movement does not trigger results.

### Five-week map

- **Nov 2–9 (prove the core):** webcam and tracking on the target device; choose vocabulary; confirm licenses; extract landmarks for a subset; train a first model.
- **Nov 9–16:** model in browser; attempt capture; ranked results with reference videos; unknown handling.
- **Nov 16–30:** lookup filters; evaluation on held-out signers; accessibility; peer-review build.
- **Nov 30–Dec 7:** fixes, public deploy, breakdown and debrief.

### Stretch goals

1. Learn mode: reference video, practice recording, side-by-side compare.
2. LLM re-ranker, only if filters fall short: reorders the model's top candidates using typed context and cannot add signs. If built, it runs in the browser to stay free and private.
3. Review and annotation of saved attempts.
4. Uploaded-video recognition; personal word list.

### Risks

- **Model accuracy** may be low. Early test: first held-out-signer score in week 1.
- **Licensing** may restrict trained weights or reference videos. Early test: read license texts; contact maintainers if unclear.
- **Webcam domain gap:** training clips differ from live input. Test live early.
- **Compute:** landmark extraction is slow. Use a vocabulary subset.
- **LLM download size and speed** if the in-browser re-ranker is added. It is optional and can be dropped.
- **Overclaiming:** the tool recognizes isolated signs; it is not translation.
